The Mission

The EU Mission for 100 Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities by 2030 is one of the most ambitious initiatives of the European Green Deal. It supports cities in their transition to climate neutrality, not as isolated actors, but as hubs of innovation and experimentation.

What is the EU Cities Mission?

The Mission was established by the European Commission under the Horizon Europe programme, with the aim of supporting at least 100 European cities in achieving climate neutrality by 2030.

Cities participating in the Mission are called not only to accelerate the drastic reduction of their emissions, but also to act as hubs of innovation and experimentation, generating solutions and practices that can be more widely adopted by European cities in their pathway towards climate neutrality by 2050.

The Mission is not just about targets. It is about changing how cities plan, finance, and deliver climate action — and about creating the conditions for systemic change.

Key Facts

  • 100+ European cities
  • 2030 climate neutrality target
  • Horizon Europe funding
  • 12 Greek cities involved

What is NetZeroCities?

NetZeroCities (NZC) implements the Mission Platform and provides participating cities with technical, regulatory and financial support, tools, methodologies and specialised expertise. Its aim is to address the structural, institutional, financial and cultural barriers that hinder the green transition.

Of particular importance is strengthening cooperation among cities and establishing mechanisms for the exchange of knowledge and experience, so that available solutions can be translated into implementable policies and investment actions at the local level.

Within this framework, SGA2 Task 1.6 – Sub-European Peer-to-Peer Collaboration is a specialised NetZeroCities action focusing on strengthening cooperation among cities at national and interregional level. In Greece, it reinforces the National-Local Dialogue between Central Government and Local Government.

Climate City Contracts (CCCs)

A key instrument of the Mission is the Climate City Contract (CCC), through which each participating city sets out its policy and social commitments, its integrated Action Plan for achieving climate neutrality, as well as the corresponding Investment Strategy.

While not legally binding, CCCs represent a clear and highly visible political commitment — not only to the European Commission, national and regional authorities, but also to the citizens they serve.

In Greece, all six Mission Cities have submitted their CCCs and are in the process of implementing their action plans.

CCC Components

  • Policy & social commitments
  • Integrated Action Plan
  • Investment Strategy
  • Monitoring & reporting

The Six Greek Mission Cities

Six Greek cities are part of the EU Mission for 100 Climate-Neutral and Smart Cities. All have submitted their Climate City Contracts and are working towards climate neutrality by 2030.

Athens

Capital city, leading on energy efficiency, mobility, and green infrastructure.

Thessaloniki

Second-largest city, focusing on transport transformation and low-emission zones.

Ioannina

Northwestern city, working on RES, energy communities, and sustainable mobility.

Kalamata

Southern city, host of the 1st National Ecosystem Event (November 2026).

Kozani

Energy transition region, focusing on district heating and RES.

Trikala

Smart city pioneer, working on energy communities and PV parks.

In parallel, Limassol (Cyprus) participates through the ClimaNet network, strengthening cross-border cooperation.

5 Core Operational Focus Areas

Based on the frequency of occurrence, the importance attributed to them by the Mission Cities, and the cross-cutting nature of the required interventions, five areas emerge as Core Operational Focus Areas:

1

Energy Efficiency and Decarbonisation of the Built Environment

Energy renovation of residential and tertiary sectors, municipal buildings, schools, and sports facilities.

2

Climate Finance and Investment Mobilisation

Connecting municipalities with banks, ESCOs, PPPs, and mechanisms for leveraging private capital.

3

Data, Monitoring and Digital Infrastructure for Climate Action

Access to and utilisation of data on buildings, energy, mobility, and urban infrastructure.

4

Technical Assistance, Capacity Building and Project Maturation

Strengthening technical capacity, accelerating project maturation, and developing common standards.

5

National–Local Governance and Regulatory Frameworks

Clarifying roles between national, regional, and local levels, and removing institutional barriers.

Key Findings from the Greek Mission Cities

The report "Common Challenges & National Support Needs for Greek Mission Cities" (December 2025) provides a systematic analysis of the barriers and opportunities identified by the six Greek Mission Cities.

Systemic, not local

The obstacles cities face are not primarily local. They are systemic: fragmented funding, regulatory gaps, data gaps, unclear mandates.

Energy efficiency first

Energy efficiency in the residential and tertiary sector received the highest relevance score (18/18) and has the greatest CO₂ reduction potential.

Data is critical

Access to building stock and energy consumption data is a prerequisite for planning and financing retrofits at scale.

Financing needs reform

Current schemes (e.g. Exoikonomo) are insufficient in scale and not adjusted to local income realities.

Municipal capacity gaps

Municipal teams often lack in-house expertise to plan and deliver complex retrofits and RES projects.

National support is key

National action can unlock progress across multiple cities simultaneously — from data sharing to regulatory updates.
